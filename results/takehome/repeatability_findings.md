## Evidence collected

- Stored Goal 5 seed-0 repeats start with identical sampled biases and random actions. Only observation features 10–15 (normalized force and torque) differ at time zero. The largest initial normalized difference is about 7.45e-9. Source: repeatability_initial_fields.json.
- The same compiled simulator called three times with identical keys and no learning also differs. Initial positions, velocities, site positions and bias forces are identical, while constraint forces, accelerations and force/torque sensors differ. The three ten-episode diagnostic batches score 8, 7 and 7 successes. Source: repeatability/a/result.json and repeatability_probe_summary.json.
- Setting XLA deterministic operations, a fixed cuBLAS workspace and synchronous CUDA launches does not remove the differences. These diagnostic batches score 7, 6 and 7 successes. Source: repeatability/execution_flags/result.json.
- The original unchanged policy and original peg_insertion/rollout.py also vary when repeated at the original test seed 123 and size 500: 303/500, 309/500 and 307/500 (60.6%, 61.8%, 61.4%). All three have finite statistics and zero detected through-wall episodes. These do not replace the Goal 1 reference. Source: repeatability/original_evaluator/result.json.

This establishes a source of variation in the underlying simulator before any new learner acts, including with the original evaluator. It rules out separate-process startup or learner updates as the sole cause. The active MuJoCo 3.15.0 bundled Warp backend uses floating-point atomic additions in constraint-force accumulation (mujoco/mjx/third_party/mujoco_warp/_src/solver.py around line 1929). The measurements are consistent with rounding differences from unordered GPU accumulation, which then grow during contact-rich trajectories. They do not establish that every later source of variation has been isolated.

MuJoCo documents GPU nondeterminism from atomic ordering in its [Warp documentation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjwarp/index.html#features). Warp's [deterministic execution documentation](https://nvidia.github.io/warp/v1.19/user_guide/execution_and_performance/deterministic_execution.html) describes an execution option for supported atomic patterns, with limitations; the installed Warp 1.18.0 provides that option too.

Enabling RUN_TO_RUN before importing physics modules makes all recorded reset arrays match exactly across three calls. However, the full trajectories still differ, and the run emits 884 deterministic-counter-overflow messages. This candidate is **invalid for adoption**: the default record bound of one is insufficient for repeated contact writes in a kernel. Its diagnostic success counts (7, 8, 8 of 10) are retained as failed-attempt evidence, not as a valid experiment. Source: repeatability/warp_deterministic/result.json and repeatability_warp.log. Compilation plus probes took 452.50 seconds, including a 353.44-second collision-kernel compilation.

A second probe increases only Warp's internal deterministic record buffer to 64 records per thread; it does not alter MuJoCo's contact capacity, geometry, physics parameters, inputs or tests. The collision code can write multiple contacts per thread (collision_primitive.py includes loops of eight contact writes, and collision_core.py increments the shared contact counter). It finishes with zero overflow messages and identical reset arrays, but full trajectories still differ: the first changed next observation and force occur at zero-based step index 117 when comparing repeat 0 with repeats 1/2, and index 135 between repeats 1 and 2. Proposed actions and true biases remain identical. Its three success counts are 8, 7 and 7 of 10. It therefore fails the full-repeatability gate and is not adopted. Source: repeatability/warp_records64/result.json and repeatability_warp64.log; total runtime 420.53 seconds.

A final candidate uses Warp's stronger GPU_TO_GPU ordering mode with the same 64-record buffer. It finishes in 411.10 seconds with zero overflow messages and exactly matching resets, but full trajectories again differ at zero-based force/next-observation index 117. Its diagnostic success counts are 7, 6 and 8 of 10. This candidate also fails the repeatability gate. No 500-episode candidate validation was launched because neither candidate passed the preceding full-trajectory gate. Source: repeatability/warp_strict/result.json and repeatability_candidate_check.json.
