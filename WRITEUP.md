# Learning Tight Peg Insertion with Reinforcement Learning and a Wrist Force Sensor

I asked how well a robot arm can learn to push a square peg into a square hole as the gap around the peg gets smaller, and whether a wrist force sensor helps when the gap is very small. I built the task in MuJoCo Playground, a GPU physics simulator, with a Franka Panda arm that already holds a 20 mm peg above a 30 mm deep hole. The robot moves the peg tip in small steps, and it sees the hole position with a hidden error of about 0.75 mm, like a camera that is slightly off. I trained the robot with PPO, a standard reinforcement learning method, on my RTX 5070 laptop GPU. Each training run took about 5.5 minutes, and I ran 26 runs over three gap sizes (2 mm, 1 mm and 0.5 mm per side), with and without the force sensor, using 3 to 5 random seeds for each case and 500 test episodes per run. Before any learning, a hand written controller that knew the true hole position inserted the peg every time at all three gaps, so the physics was sound. With position sensing only, the learned success rate fell from 99% at 2 mm to 48% at 1 mm and 17% at 0.5 mm. With the force sensor, success at 0.5 mm was 69%, which is 52 points higher (permutation test over seeds, p = 0.044). The biggest difference was in whether training worked at all: at 0.5 mm, 4 of 5 force runs learned to insert, but only 1 of 5 position runs did. Runs that failed learned to hover near the hole instead of pushing in, and results varied a lot between seeds, so one run is not enough to judge a method. I also had to fix two training problems on the way, a learning rate that caused collapse and an alignment reward that did not reward millimetre accuracy, and I applied both fixes to every condition. The main lesson is that touch makes tight insertion much easier to learn, but the learning itself is fragile, and a next step is to start training with a wide gap and slowly make it tighter.
